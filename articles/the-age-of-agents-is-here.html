The Age of Agents Is Here — Apple Is Selling the Hardware for It — Signet Article #032

The Forge — Armory Forge Systems — Signet Article #032

On Tuesday, Apple announced the new Mac mini. The specs were the specs — a faster chip, a smaller footprint, a price that went from $799 to $899. Then you read the press release and stop.

Apple, the most mainstream technology company on earth, spent the announcement calling its little desktop computer an "always-on agentic device."

Not "a powerful new computer." An always-on agentic device. Apple is telling millions of people: the machine you keep at home or in the office is now the place where AI agents live and work.

The quote that makes you pause

"Whether it's being used as a home computer, powering a professional studio, or as an always-on agentic device, it's the little Mac that can do it all," said Johny Srouji, Apple's chief hardware officer.

Read that again. Apple's hardware chief just described a desktop computer as an agentic device in a press release read by millions. The company that built its name on approachable technology just made "run AI agents 24/7" a feature of an $899 box.

The chips are the point

The new Mac mini ships with two chips: the M6 — Apple's first 2nm chip — and the M5 Pro. The M6 delivers up to 4x faster AI performance than the previous generation, with a dual 16-core Neural Engine and neural accelerators in every GPU core. The M5 Pro goes up to 64GB of unified memory with 307GB/s of bandwidth — enough to run large local models without ever touching a cloud.

But here's what Apple actually said the machine is for: "always-on, deskside agentic computing." Not better spreadsheets. Agents.

They're talking about OpenClaw

The coverage made it even more explicit. Reuters reported that the new Mac mini and Mac Studio are aimed at "consumers looking to host AI agents remotely" — and that demand has been so strong it depleted inventories at several Apple stores. CNBC put it plainly: developers using software like OpenClaw to build agents "often prefer to run them on a dedicated Mac Mini computer instead of in the cloud."

OpenClaw. In CNBC. In a story about an Apple product launch.

And Apple's own announcement imagery shows a Mac mini running Perplexity — right there in the official press materials. Apple didn't show you a calculator next to its "agentic" headline. They showed you an agent running on the machine.

The moment that matters

For two years, AI agents were a developer conversation. A niche. Something to argue about online. Apple doesn't move first — Apple moves when a thing has arrived. When Apple's press release says "agentic device," and Reuters says people are draining stores to run OpenClaw, and Perplexity is in the official product photography, the age of agents is not coming. It's here. Apple is selling the hardware for it.

What this means for your business
